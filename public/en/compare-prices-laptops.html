How to compare laptop prices across stores

Reviewed by the Lumu team · September 30, 2026

Two laptops with the same family name can have different processors, screens or memory. Match the full model number before comparing price.

Build a specification checklist

  • Manufacturer model number or SKU.
  • Exact processor model, not just “Core i7” or “Ryzen 7.”
  • RAM capacity and whether it is upgradeable.
  • Storage capacity and type.
  • Display size, resolution and relevant panel specifications.
  • Graphics chip, operating system and keyboard layout.

Missing attributes should remain unknown. A seller's generic product photo does not confirm the configuration you will receive.

Separate the purchase conditions

Keep new, refurbished and open-box units in separate groups. Check who sells and ships the laptop, what warranty applies and whether returns carry restrictions. A marketplace listing is not necessarily sold by the marketplace itself.

Compare checkout cost

Add known delivery and fees to the stated price. Confirm destination tax at checkout. If a price requires a membership, student eligibility, coupon or financing, record that condition beside it.

A cheaper listing with less RAM or a lower-resolution screen is a different value choice; it is not proof that you found the identical laptop for less.

Use your workload to narrow the search

List the software you run, portability requirements, external display needs and storage expectations. Consult the software vendor's requirements. Then compare laptops that meet those constraints rather than assuming the highest advertised processor tier is the best fit.

Search and verify

Enter the model number plus RAM and storage in Lumu. Review the matched title and confirmed fields, then verify the full specification with the seller. This guide provides a comparison method, not current prices or a performance benchmark.
