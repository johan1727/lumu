New, refurbished or open-box: compare electronics fairly

Lumu editorial guide · September 30, 2026

A refurbished device can have a lower listed price than a new one, but the two listings may include different warranties, accessories and condition guarantees. Keep those differences visible instead of sorting everything into one cheapest-price list.

Read the condition description

New, used, refurbished and open-box are useful starting labels. They do not supply a universal promise about battery capacity, cosmetic wear, testing or included parts. Read the seller's specific description for that item.

For refurbished products, identify who performed the refurbishment and what was tested or replaced. For open-box products, confirm whether the unit was returned, displayed or simply opened, and whether anything is missing. A grade such as excellent describes the seller's grading system unless a standard is explicitly stated.

Keep the exact model fixed

Match model number, generation, storage, memory and network compatibility before comparing condition. A refurbished newer generation and a new older generation are separate product choices. A phone with a carrier lock is also different from an unlocked phone, even when the product name matches.

For laptops, check the exact processor, RAM, storage and screen. For headphones, confirm that charging cases, cables and ear tips are included where applicable. The photo alone may not describe the supplied unit.

Record the differences that affect your decision

QuestionWhat to record
ConditionSeller definition, cosmetic wear and tested functions
BatteryStated capacity guarantee or unknown
WarrantyWho provides it, duration and exclusions
ReturnsTime window, item eligibility and any stated costs
Included itemsCharger, case, cables and packaging
Total costProduct, shipping, tax and confirmed required fees

Compare separate groups first

Find the best comparable offer among new units, then do the same for refurbished or open-box units. Afterward, compare the groups with their differences intact. This makes the tradeoff understandable: how much do you save, and what warranty, condition or accessories change?

For illustration, if one unit is $40 cheaper but omits an accessory you need to buy, subtract that extra expense from the apparent saving. This is a calculation method, not a recommendation for a particular seller.

When details are missing

Ask the seller before buying. Do not infer battery health from a condition label or assume the manufacturer's warranty transfers to every resale item. Save the listing terms relevant to your decision and check them again at checkout.

In Lumu, use a query with your required condition and exact variant. Treat a result with incomplete condition or warranty details as a reference requiring confirmation, not an equivalent bargain.
